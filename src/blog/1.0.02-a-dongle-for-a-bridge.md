atiic's drivers only need a bus that can write and read bytes. On a microcontroller that is its I2C peripheral. From a computer, and especially from a browser, something has to stand in between. This is the story of what does.

## The bridge that wasn't needed

The first version of i2c.ninja had its own bridge, an STC 8-bit microcontroller with an AT command set, [slow and not always steady](/blog/reloaded#the-first-try). The restart began with a question about a cheap USB dongle, the CH341T, which has an I2C mode:

> I know ch341t can work as a uart-i2c bridge, but I have never test that. I have a ch341t(i2c mode) attached to [a test machine], and its i2c end connected to a mpu6050. can you probe it?

It answered, and the MPU-6050 on the other end answered through it. That settled more than the probe:

> I have built a uart-i2c bridge on a stc 51 mcu before, which is deprecated long ago. If I would build it again, I would use wch chips. Since wch ch341t can do the bridge job. I may skip making our own bridge at all. and use ch341t instead.

The deciding reason: a browser can open the CH341 itself, over WebUSB. No bridge firmware, no driver to install, nothing between the page and the chip but a USB cable and four wires.

> Since, this approach would work with webUSB. I think I would go this path. It is not about building a product on it. It is basically about testing i2c devices drivers. And web app like ble.i2c.ninja. (https://i2c.ninja) can visualize the readings from these sensors.

For atiic the dongle is just one adapter. It drives the same bus interface a microcontroller would, so the drivers don't know or care.

## Getting the browser to the dongle

WebUSB is simple once it works, and every operating system has its own reason why it doesn't at first. That became [Get started](/get-started), written for someone who only wants to use the website:

- **Linux** keeps USB devices for root unless a udev rule hands the dongle to the person at the keyboard. And the kernel's own `spi-ch341` driver claims the dongle on every plug; the page shows how to let go of it.
- **Windows** lets a browser open only devices on the WinUSB driver, so the dongle has to be switched to it once.
- **Android** simply works: "I have tried i2c.ninja on android chrome with OTG, it works."

## Sensors, one at a time

With the path open, the sensors came back as they were found and wired up, each only once its driver had passed on the board and its page on the bench: the [MPU-6050](/mpu6050) first, then the [SHT20](/sht20) and [AHT21B](/aht21) for humidity, the [VL53L1X](/vl53l1x) for distance, the [INA231](/ina231) for power ("I believe it is a ina231, check the old atiic driver"), the [MAX30102](/max30102) for a heartbeat in a fingertip, the [MLX90614](/mlx90614) for temperature at a distance. At some point:

> Okay, I think i am running out of sensors. :)
