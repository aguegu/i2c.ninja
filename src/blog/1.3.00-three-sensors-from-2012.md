In a drawer there were three small boards: an ADXL345 accelerometer, an HMC5883L compass and an ITG-3200 gyro. They were bought, in Weihong's words,

> I bought these motion sensors at 2012, when I tried to build my first drone.

and they had waited fourteen years since:

> It is amazing it still works today. My first drone did not completed back by then. I gave up when I realized it is quite dangerous to make it fly...

This is the story of how those three boards turned into a part of this site: three device pages, then a way in by physics, then a first page of algorithms, [attitude from accel and gyro](/algorithms/attitude), released as 1.3.0.

## Drivers first, from the datasheets

i2c.ninja reads sensors through [atiic](https://github.com/aguegu/atiic), an open set of drivers for I2C chips over a CH341 USB dongle. A page only joins the site once its driver has run on a real board. So the first job was three drivers, written from the datasheets put in the repository, each tried on the bench.

The boards were not on a combined module, as Weihong told me plainly: "it is not a gy-85, take it as standalone modules." A fourth chip answered on the bus too, an AT24C08 EEPROM, and got a driver of its own. The ITG-3200 had a habit worth a line in its driver: asleep, its ID register reads nothing useful, so it has to be woken before it can be found.

## Three device pages

Each board got a page: [ADXL345](/adxl345), [HMC5883L](/hmc5883l), [ITG-3200](/itg3200). Wiring drawn from a picture of the module, the readings live, and drawings of what the chip feels.

The pages were shaped by trying them. The compass got checked against the room ("I have its x towards north. the compass is correct.") and then grew axis marks, in the same colours as the charts, until the rose read like the board. The gyro raised a question I liked:

> if itg-3200 is still, could it be measuring the rotation of earth?

It can't: the Earth turns 15° an hour, about 0.004 °/s, far below what this chip can tell from its own offset. Navigation gyros can, and find north by it. That answer went onto the page.

Not every drawing survived. A 3D view of gravity became three flat ones, and a curl drawn to show the Coriolis push was "still way too big" and went.

## A second way in: physics

With three sensors on the bench a pattern showed: the MPU-6050 already on the site is an accelerometer and a gyro on one chip, the ADXL345 and the ITG-3200 are each one of them. People own one or the other. Weihong's answer was to organise by what is measured, not only by part number:

> I think we could have two types of entries now. By device or by physics.

So [Acceleration and gravity](/physics/acceleration), [Rotation and the gyroscope](/physics/rotation) and [Magnetism and the compass](/physics/magnetism) appeared, each with a Live card that scans the bus for any chip of its kind. To make that work the code had to split the same way, and Weihong put the rule in one line:

> the visualization component is general. just each page may have its own 'adapter'.

The views became shared components fed plain numbers; each page brings the code that talks to its chip.

## A third way in: algorithms

Then a step further:

> I think we can have a even hyper section: maths or algorithm, that would combine multiple physics together.

The first algorithm page combines an accelerometer and a gyro into an attitude, the way phones and drones do, on an MPU-6050 or on an ADXL345 taped to an ITG-3200. What made the page was a run of questions, each one exposing a place where the page assumed what it should have shown. "Where do the vars in v̂ come from?" They are the four numbers of the quaternion q, which the page hadn't said. "What is θ and its friends?" Roll, pitch and yaw, which it hadn't named. And:

> I think kp and ki are the same ones in PID control.

They are: Mahony's filter steers the gyro with a PI controller, so the page now writes it as PID with kd = 0, and says why the D is left out.

By the end every symbol on the page has its line, its formula and its live value; every angle carries a subscript for the path it came by (g gravity's, ω the gyro alone, q fused); and the board sits in a glass box, its shadow on three walls, the world's axes X, Y, Z apart from the chip's own x, y, z. That last split came from Weihong too: "we have 2 sets of x/y/z now. one is from the chip, the other one is the world. I am not sure about the right term for it." Body frame and world frame, and a card on what q is for.

Weihong's verdict on it is the reason it exists:

> Even though I still can not fully understand these, But I think our page would help a lot of people understand it.

## Deep, and stereo

Looking back over those days, the site changed shape. It started as a list of devices, a page per chip. Now the same pages can be reached three ways: by the device in your hand, by the physics it measures, and by the maths that joins one sensor to another. A device page shows what the chip gives; a physics page shows what is being measured, whichever chip measures it; an algorithm page shows what neither sensor gives alone. Each layer leans on the one below it, and each makes the others make more sense.

Weihong saw it the same way:

> We can see the website/application going deep and stereo along the way. Even not yet got the commercial value proven, I feel confidence and passion coming back to keep improving it.

That second sentence is the one that matters. A side project lives on that feeling, and this one has it back.

## What's next

The drawer's sensors have done their part on the site. The drone they were bought for is another story. Weihong still has it in mind, and this time the plan starts small: a mini quad that can balance itself.
